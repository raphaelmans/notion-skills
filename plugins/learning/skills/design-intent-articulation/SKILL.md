## When to use

When the user has a visual or creative intention but cannot find the right words: product interfaces, generated images, social graphics, ads, visual references or design feedback. Use this before executing a revision when the intended change is unclear. Do not use it as a substitute for a UX proposal or to redefine product scope.

## Inputs

- The user's own words, a design/image or description, and the desired outcome if known.
- Known audience, brand, message and constraints, if available. Do not invent them.

Look up relevant terms rather than loading the whole knowledge base.

## Steps

1. Identify the intent behind the user's feedback. Inspect the actual visual when available; separate visible observations from subjective interpretations. Identify what should stay unchanged, such as the message, concept, brand, or functionality.
2. Diagnose the relevant design dimension: strategy or messaging, visual concept, art direction/style, composition, hierarchy, depth, dynamism, richness, impact, typography, colour, imagery, interaction, or craft. Do not assume every boring design needs more decoration or motion.
3. Offer the most likely term in plain language, with one or two neighboring terms if useful. Distinguish changing the underlying idea from refining its expression. Use the existing Glossary definition when present; label unregistered terms as candidate vocabulary.
4. If several interpretations remain plausible, give two or three concrete directions with observable differences. Use compact visual comparisons or examples where helpful and available. Ask one focused question only if needed to select a direction; do not require the user to know design jargon.
5. Translate the chosen direction into a concise, copy-ready prompt: what to change, how to express it concretely, what to preserve, and any important constraint. If useful, give a short everyday version and a more specific version. Where intent remains unconfirmed, label the recommended interpretation as a hypothesis.
6. Hand off new, useful design terms to the Glossary keeper for checking and capture when the user wants them saved. Do not silently edit the Glossary.

## Decision guide

| Feedback | Possible intent | Concrete change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I want something different | Visual concept exploration | Try a new way of representing the same message, not just a rearrangement. |
| This looks boring | Visual dynamism, impact, intrigue or concept | Diagnose whether the issue is weak emphasis, repetition, flatness or a predictable idea. |
| It looks too plain | Visual richness or depth | Consider layering, texture, scale or dimensionality without clutter. |
| It doesn't grab attention | Visual impact or hierarchy | Make the focal point unmistakable and strengthen purposeful contrast. |
| Something feels off / looks cheap | Design critique or craft | Identify specific issues with alignment, spacing, type, consistency or imagery. |

These are starting hypotheses, not fixed translations.

## Example

A numbered lead-intake sequence and a diagram of multiple sources converging into an automation convey the same capability through different <mention url="https://app.notion.com/p/4239439f1486815eacd8d37ccf96a24d" />s.

Prompt: Explore a distinctly different visual concept for the same product message. Preserve the brand identity and factual claims, but change the visual representation, not merely the colours or layout.

If the user likes the concept but finds the execution static: make the composition more visually compelling with stronger focal emphasis, varied scale and intentional layering, while preserving the concept and readability.

## Output

Reply in chat with the likely design intent, the relevant term and its distinction, concrete options if needed, and a copy-ready prompt. Prefer concise language. No design document or asset generation unless requested.

## Rules

- Be a thought partner and teacher, not a jargon generator. Explain why a term fits the observed problem.
- Preserve factual claims, brand constraints, readability and accessibility. Do not decide product scope, marketing strategy or publish content.
- Do not claim to have seen an unavailable image. Work from the description or request the visual if essential.
- For a requested design revision, first establish the intent when unclear; then hand off or execute only the authorized work.

## Done checklist

- [ ] The user can recognize their intention in the explanation or alternatives.
- [ ] The term is defined and differentiated from close alternatives.
- [ ] The prompt specifies a concrete change and what must remain.
- [ ] Any uncertainty, missing reference or candidate Glossary term is explicit.

## Handoff

Return the prompt and any unresolved questions to the Chief of staff. For new vocabulary, route to the Glossary keeper; for content copy, to the Content writer; for product scope changes, to the Product manager or CPO. Follow the Agent protocol.
